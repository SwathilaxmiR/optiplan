# Walkthrough: Physical-Layer QoT Engine & Modulation Formats

## 1. What It Does
This module forms the ground-truth physical layer of OptiPlan:
- **`IQoTModel`**: Abstract strategy interface for evaluating Quality of Transmission along lightpath routes without coupling to network state or routing logic.
- **`GNModel`**: High-performance analytical implementation of the closed-form Gaussian Noise (GN) model for dispersion-unmanaged coherent Nyquist-WDM systems (Poggiolini et al., 2012). It calculates:
  - Amplified Spontaneous Emission (ASE) noise power accumulated across in-line EDFA spans.
  - Optical Signal-to-Noise Ratio (OSNR) in standard $12.5\text{ GHz}$ ($0.1\text{ nm}$) reference bandwidth.
  - Nonlinear Interference (NLI) power generated by Cross-Phase Modulation (XPM) and Self-Phase Modulation (SPM) as a function of occupied channels on traversed links.
  - Multi-span accumulation with coherence factor $\epsilon \approx 0.05$.
  - Effective linear and logarithmic Signal-to-Noise Ratio ($\text{SNR}$).
  - Analytical optimum launch power $P_{\text{opt}} = (P_{\text{ASE}} / 2\eta)^{1/3}$.
- **`ModulationFormat`**: Object-oriented hierarchy (`DP_BPSK`, `DP_QPSK`, `DP_8QAM`, `DP_16QAM`) providing bit rates at $32\text{ GBaud}$ ($50, 100, 150, 200\text{ Gb/s}$) and calculating required SNR thresholds from pre-FEC BER limits (default $10^{-2}$) and design margins.

---

## 2. Why It Is Designed This Way
- **Strategy Pattern (`IQoTModel`)**: Decouples network planning from physical-layer modeling. In Phase 5, an ML QoT surrogate (`MLQoTModel`) or a fixed reach table can implement `IQoTModel` interchangeably with `GNModel`.
- **Pure Functions & Const-Correctness**: `evaluatePath` is completely stateless and side-effect free. Given a route segment vector and configuration, it performs pure numerical computation. This enables lock-free parallel candidate evaluation across multiple threads in Phase 4.
- **Strict SI Unit Conversion**: Internal calculations convert all parameters ($\alpha_{\text{dB}}$, $\beta_2$, $\gamma$, frequencies) to standard MKS units (meters, seconds, Watts, Hz) upfront. This eliminates unit mismatch bugs that frequently plague optical networking tools.
- **Closed-Form GN vs. Split-Step Fourier**: While split-step Fourier simulation solves the nonlinear Schrödinger equation directly, it requires minutes to hours per lightpath. The closed-form GN model evaluates in microseconds while maintaining high accuracy for dispersion-unmanaged links.

## 4. Likely Interview Questions & Answers

### Q1: Why does nonlinear interference (NLI) depend on the current network state, and why is this critical for dynamic network planning?
**Answer:**
In uncompensated coherent optical transmission, the Kerr nonlinearity causes optical channels to interact through Cross-Phase Modulation (XPM) and Four-Wave Mixing (FWM). In the GN model, the NLI power spectral density $G_{\text{NLI}}$ scales with the total occupied bandwidth $B_{\text{WDM}} = N_{\text{ch}} \cdot \Delta f$ through the term $\text{asinh}\left(\frac{\pi^2}{2} |\beta_2| L_{\text{eff,a}} B_{\text{WDM}}^2\right)$.
When a new lightpath is provisioned on a link, $N_{\text{ch}}$ increases, which adds NLI noise not only to the new channel but to **all existing channels sharing that link**. A naive planner that evaluates lightpaths in isolation risks degrading existing live traffic below their FEC thresholds. OptiPlan must re-validate all co-propagating lightpaths before committing a reservation.

### Q2: Why does the SNR curve peak at an optimum launch power $P_{\text{opt}}$, and what is the relationship between ASE and NLI at that point?
**Answer:**
Linear ASE noise $P_{\text{ASE}}$ generated by optical amplifiers is independent of signal launch power $P_{\text{ch}}$. In contrast, fiber nonlinear interference power scales cubically with signal launch power ($P_{\text{NLI}} = \eta \cdot P_{\text{ch}}^3$).
The effective SNR is given by:
$$\text{SNR} = \frac{P_{\text{ch}}}{P_{\text{ASE}} + \eta P_{\text{ch}}^3}$$
At low launch power, $P_{\text{ASE}} \gg P_{\text{NLI}}$ and SNR increases linearly with power ($+1\text{ dB/dB}$). At high launch power, $P_{\text{NLI}} \gg P_{\text{ASE}}$ and SNR decreases with power ($-2\text{ dB/dB}$).
Differentiating the inverse SNR with respect to $P_{\text{ch}}$ yields:
$$\frac{d}{dP_{\text{ch}}}\left(\frac{P_{\text{ASE}}}{P_{\text{ch}}} + \eta P_{\text{ch}}^2\right) = -\frac{P_{\text{ASE}}}{P_{\text{ch}}^2} + 2\eta P_{\text{ch}} = 0 \implies P_{\text{opt}} = \left(\frac{P_{\text{ASE}}}{2\eta}\right)^{1/3}$$
Substituting $P_{\text{opt}}$ into $P_{\text{NLI}}$ reveals that $P_{\text{NLI}} = \eta P_{\text{opt}}^3 = \frac{1}{2} P_{\text{ASE}}$. Thus, maximum transmission SNR occurs precisely when nonlinear noise power is **$3\text{ dB}$ below** linear ASE noise power ($1/3$ nonlinear vs. $2/3$ linear noise balance).

### Q3: What is the significance of the multi-span coherence factor $\epsilon$, and why not assume simple incoherent power summation ($\epsilon = 0$)?
**Answer:**
A strictly incoherent GN model assumes that NLI generated in separate spans is mutually uncorrelated, meaning total NLI power accumulates linearly with span count: $P_{\text{NLI}} \propto N_s^1$.
However, in dispersion-unmanaged links, the self-phase modulation (SPM) component retains partial phase correlation across adjacent spans because chromatic dispersion does not completely randomize the phase over short span distances. As shown by Carena et al. (2014) and Poggiolini et al. (2014), modeling multi-span accumulation with a slight coherence factor $N_s^{1+\epsilon}$ (with $\epsilon \approx 0.05 - 0.06$) accurately accounts for this constructive interference, preventing the model from underestimating nonlinear degradation on multi-span paths without resorting to the computationally expensive integral evaluations of the full EGN model.
